Platform as a product

Start with users and friction, not a Kubernetes shopping list.

By the endWrite a platform product hypothesis with users, problem, capability, and measure.
01 / Mental model

Build the right picture first

A platform is a curated set of capabilities delivered through self-service contracts. Platform engineering is the discipline of researching, building, and operating that product for internal developers.

The interface is the product

Developers consume documented APIs, templates, CLIs, portals, and paved workflows—not your internal tool topology.

Golden paths are optional defaults

They make the safe common case easy while allowing justified escape hatches. A mandated bottleneck is not self-service.

Adoption is evidence

Provisioning time, deployment success, support load, cognitive load, and voluntary use reveal whether value is real.

02 / Worked example

Product hypothesis

ANNOTATED EXAMPLEread → predict → explain
For product teams shipping HTTP services,
who lose days assembling delivery infrastructure,
we provide a self-service service template and environment API
that produces an owned, observable, policy-compliant service in 30 minutes.
We will know it works when adoption rises and lead time/support tickets fall.
This statement is falsifiable. It names a thin starting slice and measures outcomes rather than tool installation.
03 / Deliberate practice

Do the work

Predict the result before each step. Keep a small note of what surprised you; surprise is useful evidence that your mental model changed.

  1. Interview one developer about their last deployment.
  2. Map waiting, handoffs, rework, and hidden expertise.
  3. Write one hypothesis and baseline measure.
  4. Name one thing the first platform version will not solve.
04 / Retrieval

Close the notes

What separates a platform from shared infrastructure?
Reveal answer

A deliberately designed, supported, and measured self-service experience for defined users—not merely centrally operated technology.
